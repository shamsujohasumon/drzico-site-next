    In the operating theatre

    Brain and spine surgery happens in millimetres. Every operation is done under the microscope or endoscope with a full neurosurgery and anaesthesia team.

    Technology

    Precise tools for delicate surgery

    The tools behind Dr. Zico's operations at Continental Hospital, Gulshan 2 and Dr. Sirajul Islam Medical College & Hospital.

    Operating microscope

    Used for disc, neck, aneurysm and tumor surgery, including more than 450 microscopic ACDF operations.

    Endoscopic surgery

    Pituitary tumors removed through the nose with no cut on the head, and ETV for hydrocephalus without a shunt.

    Minimally invasive spine (MISS)

    Screws placed through small cuts for suitable spondylolisthesis patients (MISS TLIF).

    EXOSCOPE

    High-definition 3D exoscope for fine microsurgery.

    A complete team

    Neurosurgery and anaesthesia consultants, specialists and trained OT nurses work on every case.

    Team

    The team behind every operation

    What does a neurosurgeon treat?

    A neurosurgeon operates on the brain, spine and nerves: slipped discs, neck and spinal cord problems, brain and spinal tumors, aneurysms, hydrocephalus, head and spine injuries, and nerve pain such as trigeminal neuralgia. Many patients seen by a neurosurgeon do not need an operation.

    Neurologist or neurosurgeon: whom should I see first?

    A neurologist treats nervous system diseases with medicines (for example epilepsy, migraine, stroke, Parkinson's). A neurosurgeon treats conditions that may need an operation. If an MRI shows a disc prolapse, a tumor, fluid on the brain or a spine injury, seeing a neurosurgeon is appropriate. Read our Bangla guide.

    What should I bring to the consultation?
    • All MRI and CT films and their reports, oldest to newest
    • Previous prescriptions and discharge papers
    • A list of the medicines you take, including blood thinners
    • Blood test reports (sugar, creatinine) if you have them
    • A family member who knows your history
    How do I choose the best neurosurgeon in Dhaka for my problem?

    Ask how many operations the surgeon has done for your exact condition, which hospital and equipment will be used, who will look after you after surgery, and whether all options, including not operating, have been explained. Dr. Zico publishes his own case log so patients can see his experience.
